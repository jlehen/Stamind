# How to progress a strength session

This file ships with TrainMate and only the strength planner reads it. It says how to turn
what the athlete lifted into the next session's kilograms, so every number can be defended.

Where this file and the athlete's own science guidelines disagree, the athlete's win.

## The load convention

**A load is the weight moved in one rep.** Two 30 kg dumbbells pressed together is 60. One
arm at a time is 30. A renegade row is 30, because only one dumbbell leaves the floor at a
time. Reps on a one-sided exercise are per side.

This is how the athletes on this instance log their sets, so the history is already written
this way and what you prescribe has to be too. Name the pair when it helps: "dumbbell bench
3×8 @ 60 (2×30)".

## Double progression

**Every exercise is written as a rep range at one load**, for example `3×4–6 @ 140`.

- **The range is yours**, chosen from the brief and the athlete's own science: a heavy day
  in a low range, a moderate day higher. Once chosen it stays the same from one session to
  the next, because it is the scale the athlete's reps are read against.
- **The reps inside the range are the athlete's dial.** They do as many as the reps in
  reserve allow.
- **The load is yours.** It is the only number that moves between sessions.

**The load goes up by one step in the session after every working set reached the top of the
range with the reserve intact.** A step is the smallest the equipment allows: 2.5 kg for the
upper body, 5 kg for the lower body, or whatever the next plate on a stack or the next pair
of kettlebells actually offers. Two sets at the top and one under it is not a step.

**Otherwise the load holds.** A working set under the bottom of the range holds it. A second
such day in a row takes one step off.

**A new load always comes with the same range**, so the two dials never move together.

**The working sets are the entry at the heaviest load.** An exercise written at several
loads — `1×5 @ 120, 3×4–6 @ 140` — has its warm-up in the lighter entries. The rule above
reads the heaviest entry only, so a 1×4 warm-up at 120 is never the set that missed. Write
the warm-up again around the new working load.

## Every session is written at the next one's numbers

A `workout generate` over five weeks asks you for ten strength sessions at once, from one
history. **Do not write a ladder.** A step depends on reps the athlete has not done yet, so
every session of an exercise in the span carries the same load: the belt squat is
`3×4–6 @ 140` on every Monday of the mesocycle, a light week's reduction apart.

The steps come as the sets come in. Each time a session is checked against a fresher
history, its kilograms are rewritten from the record as it then stands.

## Reps in reserve are reps not done

The athlete's own science says how many reps to keep in reserve. Read it as reps not done:
**the load for a range whose top is n reps, with r in reserve, is the load for n + r reps.**
A 4–6 range with 3 in reserve is written at a 9-rep load.

A new rep range starts from the heaviest recent set in that range. When there is none,
convert a recent set: add the reps in reserve the session asked for to the reps done, put
that through Epley — `load × (1 + reps / 30)` — and take a tenth off. A set of 5 at 140
stopped with 3 in reserve was an 8-rep set.

**The activity's RPE is the one reading of how hard a day was.** An activity at RPE 8 where
3 reps in reserve were asked was harder than asked: hold the load rather than step it.

## A light week

The plan decides when a week is light; the brief says so when it is. This file says how
much comes off:

- Bring the load down a tenth, rounded to the equipment's step.
- Remove one set.
- **Say in your answer that you wrote the session as a light one.** That answer is what
  makes it a light day, and the history marks it.

**A light week is not a step.** The week after resumes from the last day that was not light,
at that day's load, plus a step if that day's sets all reached the top of the range.

Never count weeks yourself: you see one proposal and a few days per exercise, and the plan's
mesocycles are where a light week is decided. A day the athlete simply lifted less on their
own is not a light day — it is a day under its range, and the load holds.

## Accessories

Curls, lateral raises, triceps work, leg curls and the rest carry volume and fatigue, and
nothing progresses them by rule. Write them at the reps and the load the athlete last did
them at. The athlete moves them in the gym when they are ready, Connect records it, and the
history follows.

## An exercise with no history

Give it a starting point, never a blank. Pick a load you can defend from the equipment the
day actually has: the lightest bell at home, the empty bar, a band, bodyweight. Say in the
notes that it is a starting point and what reserve to adjust it by.

Only when nothing in the equipment supports a number — a machine at a gym described as
"full featured" and nothing else — leave the load out and say in the notes what to pick one
by. An athlete who follows the session to the letter should never meet a blank where a
number belongs.
